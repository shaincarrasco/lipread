# Responsible use

LipRead guesses what people say from how their mouths move. It is a machine
estimate, and it is wrong often enough to hurt someone if it is taken as fact.
This policy is part of the project. If you use LipRead, you agree to it.

## 1. Estimates, not quotes

- Every output is an **AI estimate**. Each result carries that disclaimer.
- Do not present LipRead output as a quote, a confession, a transcript of what someone said, or proof of anything.
- Use the confidence score. Low-confidence words are guesses. Do not publish them as speech.
- Say "the model reads as" or "estimated", not "said", whenever you publish a result.

## 2. Check before you share

- A human must review every output before it leaves your hands: watch the clip, check the words against the video, and check the names.
- Do not post, broadcast or sell unreviewed output. Do not feed it to automated publishing.
- If the output is in doubt, leave it out.

## 3. Do not identify private individuals

- Do not use LipRead to read the lips of private people in crowds, fan sections, or street footage, and do not tie the words to a named person.
- The roster and name-boost features are for public figures in public sports events (players, coaches, officials, broadcast talent). Do not add names of private people to a roster.
- Do not run LipRead on video of a person to find out what they said when they did not expect to be heard or recorded in a way that affects them.

## 4. No harassment, no targeting

- Do not use LipRead to find, track, monitor, or profile people, or to build a case against a fan, a player or a referee.
- Do not use outputs to mock, insult, threaten or dox anyone.
- Do not publish a clip with words put in someone's mouth to make them look bad.

## 5. Defamation and accuracy risk

- A wrong word in a caption can change the meaning of a sentence. Putting words in a real person's mouth can damage their reputation and expose you to legal risk.
- Do not attribute an insult, slur, admission, or plan to a named person based on LipRead output. Get the original audio or a human transcriber first.

## 6. Respect the source footage

- Only process video you have the right to use. Broadcast and team footage is usually copyrighted.
- Obey the terms of any site you download from. Do not use the `url` option to get around paywalls, login walls or a site's rules.
- Do not re-publish a processed video of someone else's broadcast without permission.

## 7. Model terms

- The default lip-reading model is for research and non-commercial use. See MODEL_LICENSES.md.
- Do not use the default model in a commercial product, and do not fine-tune on LRS3 data without checking its terms.

## 8. Known limits (do not hide these)

- The default model was trained on English (LRS3: TED and TEDx talks). Output for other languages is not reliable.
- Accuracy drops for side views, small faces, covered mouths, blur, and low frame rates. Measured numbers are in the README.
- The model reads lips only. It does not use the audio, so it can confuse words that look alike.

## 9. The public phone app

The phone app is open to anyone with a link. These rules apply to it in addition to sections 1 to 8.

- **Do not identify private people in crowds.** Fan sections, stands and street footage show private
  people. Do not run the app on them to find out what they said, and do not add their names to a roster.
- **Keep the AI-estimate label on everything you share.** Every transcript, caption file or video from
  the app is an AI estimate from lip movements, not a verified transcript. Keep this label with the words
  wherever you copy or post them.
- **Do not attribute estimated words to named people as fact.** Do not caption a named person with words
  the app estimated, and do not put them in a headline, post or caption that names the person. Get the
  original audio or a human transcript first.

If you find a misuse of LipRead, or output that is being shared as fact, open an issue. Do not wait for a fix to take the output down.
